Part 01

Introduce yourself

This is how you greet someone and give your name in Thai. Men and women use different personal pronouns and polite endings, so read the card that applies to you.

If you are a man
สวัสดีครับ
sà-wàt-dee kráp
Hello.
ผมชื่อ … ครับ
pŏm chûu your name kráp
My name is …
If you are a woman
สวัสดีค่ะ
sà-wàt-dee kâ
Hello.
ฉันชื่อ … ค่ะ
chăn chûu your name kâ
My name is …
You are a…
Part 02

The wai

The wai (ไหว้) is a traditional Thai greeting and a way to show respect. Instead of shaking hands, Thai people use a wai when greeting someone, saying goodbye, thanking someone, or showing respect.

There are several levels of wai depending on the situation and how much respect is being shown. As a beginner, you only need to learn this one:

  1. Put your palms together with your fingers pointing up.
  2. Bring your hands up to around your nose/face level.
  3. Give a small, polite bow with your head.
  4. Say what you want to say, for example:
สวัสดีครับ
sà-wàt-dee kráp
Hello.
ขอบคุณครับ
kòp kun kráp
Thank you.

Part 04

Ask someone's name

Tap play to hear each question, then repeat it out loud.

If you are a man
คุณชื่ออะไรครับ
kun chûu à-rai kráp
What's your name?
If you are a woman
คุณชื่ออะไรคะ
kun chûu à-rai ká
What's your name?
Good to know

In casual conversation, Thai speakers often drop คุณ kun (you) and simply ask:

ชื่ออะไร (ครับ/คะ)
chûu à-rai (kráp/ká)

Remember: ครับ kráp and คะ ká are the polite particles you met earlier in the lesson — men use one, women the other. They carry no meaning of their own, but adding them to the end of a sentence is what makes it sound polite and respectful.

Part 08

Notes

A few helpful tips for learning the Thai alphabet, using romanization, and understanding tones.

Optional extra

Learning the Thai alphabet

If you’re interested in learning the Thai alphabet, I highly recommend these free YouTube videos:

Thai consonants Thai vowels

Learning the alphabet mostly comes down to memorization and practice, so I encourage my students to study it at their own pace alongside the course. Once you’re familiar with it, we can also start doing some easy reading practice together. :-)

Knowing the Thai alphabet is not required to complete this course. Every word includes an audio recording and romanization, so it’s entirely up to you whether you would also like to learn how to read and write Thai.

Pronunciation guide

Using romanization

Romanization can be especially helpful if you decide not to learn the Thai alphabet, but try not to rely on it too heavily.

Think of it as a helpful guide, not the pronunciation itself. Listen to the audio first, then use the romanization only as a reminder.

Coming soon

Understanding tones

Thai is a tonal language. The marks in the romanization, such as à, â, and ǎ, show the tone of each word.

In an upcoming lesson, I’ll explain how Thai tones work and teach you everything you need to know. For now, let me—and the audio recordings—guide your pronunciation.
